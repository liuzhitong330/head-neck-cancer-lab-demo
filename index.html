Motivation RNA & protein Response review Relevance

In patient-derived head and neck tumors, an RNA marker is not always a substitute for measuring its protein. The laboratory’s cetuximab study makes that distinction particularly interesting: caveolin-1 and Sox-2 were more informative together at the protein level. Which individual models deserve a second assay before an RNA-defined state guides the next experiment?

I built an RNA–protein discrepancy explorer that turns the study’s paired measurements into a model-level confirmation queue. A second analysis reviews the observed treatment records, keeping the published response labels separate from an adjustable endpoint comparison.

How it works

The 2021 JCI Insight study provides paired RNA and RPPA protein profiles for 65 patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models. This processed-data reanalysis joins eight published markers by model ID, then compares each model’s position within the RNA and protein distributions. A large difference is a reason to check an assay, not evidence of a mechanism.

Percentile ranks use the full available cohort and stay fixed when you filter. Only 17 models have experimentally evaluated cetuximab response; the other 48 remain explicitly unmeasured here. No response prediction is made for them.

    Why this matters for the lab

    Moving from an expression pattern to a useful experiment requires deciding what to remeasure, in which material, and against which control. This small analysis adds an inspectable confirmation queue and an endpoint review to the published study. Individual discordant models are an inferred follow-up opportunity, not a claim about the lab’s current internal bottleneck.

    For a new cohort, the same analysis could accept an RNA matrix, a protein matrix, unique model IDs, and clinical annotations. Treatment records would additionally need model, treatment, day, and normalized growth fields. Sample identity, assay specificity, missingness, and batch effects must be checked first; ranks must be recomputed within the new reference cohort, not transferred as calibrated cutoffs.

    Methods note

    Source: Bouhaddou et al., “Caveolin-1 and Sox-2 are predictive biomarkers of cetuximab response in head and neck cancer”, JCI Insight (2021), doi:10.1172/jci.insight.151982. Supplemental Tables 2–5 supply model metadata, median-centered RPPA, growth records, and processed RNA values. Article and source data are credited to the authors under CC BY 4.0.

    Rank = 100 × (average rank − 1) / (finite assay count − 1). Absolute RNA–protein rank gaps compare within-assay ordering, not concentration or fold-change differences. SOX2/JG2 and CLDN7/JG14 contain source spreadsheet errors; those RNA values remain missing. Matching model IDs does not establish matched aliquots, identical passage, or biological replication. Bulk composition, measurement specificity, and sample handling remain possible explanations.

    The eight markers were selected in the source study, so these same data cannot independently validate them. This page does not refit the published SVM, report predictive accuracy, establish causality, or recommend human treatment. Growth records lack animal IDs; uncertainty cannot be turned into animal-level confidence intervals. Two nonfinite growth records are retained as missing in the audit.
